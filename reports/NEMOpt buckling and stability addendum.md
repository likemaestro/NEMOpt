# Certify stability before trusting NEMOpt's auxetics

NEMOpt's homogenized outputs (the Poisson's ratio ν, bulk modulus κ and shear modulus μ derived from 𝔸_M) describe a real material only while the principal branch, on which every cell deforms identically, is stable, and **no published design has been checked for this**. The refactored package should add three nested checks: a definiteness test of the reduced tangent at wave vector k = 0 that covers both point-symmetry parity sectors, a strong-ellipticity test of 𝔸_M for the long-wave limit k → 0, and Bloch–Floquet checks for modes that span several cells. All three run as read-only observers whose non-interference with the FEAD2026 profile is proven by a single bitwise gate. One-cell and half-cell results both stop being physically valid at the first load factor t_c on the ramp F(t) = I + t(F_M − I) where any check fails; the half cell reproduces the full cell's symmetric branch exactly but cannot see symmetry-breaking modes, so full/half agreement never proves stability and half-cell results must be re-diagnosed on a reconstructed full cell. Because the FEAD2026 constituent provably cannot lose ellipticity under compression, every instability is structural; the riskiest published case is §5.2 case 2, whose "tensile" load produces a compressive principal stress about 0.9 times the tensile one in a linearized isotropic idealization, followed by §5.3 case 2 with a genuine 5% principal compression. Per-evaluation checks cost under 1% of a legacy evaluation and full Bloch scans of final designs take seconds to two minutes, but the inputs of the published designs were never recorded, so every audit runs on regenerated designs. For the next paper the panel recommends constraining instability before exploiting it, using eigenvalue sensitivities that reuse the code's existing Z contraction, a pseudo-mass metric against the artificial modes of a soft-element band, and the reference clipped SIMP law as default with a smooth floor tested only as an ablation; nineteen proposed decision records, NDR-0045 to NDR-0063, carry these choices.

### The consolidated recommendation

The seven positions fit together because they act on different layers, as in the refactoring report. The theorist, the metamaterials mechanician and the group theorist define **what must be checked**: three nested tests, a validity rule that confines results to the load range below t_c, and the requirement that half-cell results be checked on the full cell. The numerical analyst and the architect define **how the checks run** without touching FEAD2026. The optimization methodologist and the V&V engineer define **how the checks become trustworthy evidence**, and later a design constraint. ## Three nested checks decide when one-cell and half-cell results stop being valid

### Microscopic instability precedes or coincides with macroscopic loss of ellipticity

The principal solution of an infinite periodic solid under prescribed F_M is stable only while Hill's second variation stays positive for Bloch-wave perturbations at every wave vector of the Brillouin zone. Geymonat, Müller and Triantafyllidis proved that the Bloch infimum obeys **β(λ) ≤ β(λ; k → 0) ≤ B(λ)**, where B = min over unit m and N of (m⊗N):𝔸̄:(m⊗N) is the macroscopic ellipticity indicator, and that loss of rank-one convexity is exactly long-wavelength microscopic buckling. Their 1993 full text could not be read, so these statements are **verified as relayed** by Zhang et al.'s restatement ([Geymonat et al. 1993](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00380256); [Zhang et al. 2021, Eqs. 55–56](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.02371)). Microscopic instability therefore precedes macroscopic instability or coincides with it. The earliest comparison, for fiber composites, found that the macroscopic approach "consistently and sometimes considerably overestimates the stable region" ([Triantafyllidis & Maker 1985](https://doi.org/10.1115/1.3169148)), and Zhang et al. state the consequence for homogenization directly: results "are only valid before the bifurcation occurs" ([Zhang et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1002/nme.6802)).

Two subtleties shape the implementation. A mode with the cell's own periodicity is attained at exactly k = 0, but a long-wavelength mode is only approached as k → 0 and the stability surface is discontinuous at the origin; a computation at k = 0 cannot see it, so the long-wave region belongs to the 𝔸_M test ([Zhang et al. 2021, §4.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.02371); verified). And Newton does not detect instability. It converges to unstable equilibria as readily as to stable ones, so the one-cell 𝔸_M, ν, κ and μ keep "converging" past t_c and certify nothing there (inference).

NEMOpt's article frames 𝔸_M as "the linear response (first order linear approximation of the response at a deformed state) of the unit cell at the state imposed by F_M" and names "instabilities that might arise particularly under compressive macroscopic loading" as open work ([Güven & Özdemir 2026, §4 and §6](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.finel.2026.104599); verified). Wallin & Tortorelli, whose formalism NEMOpt follows, state that "microscopic- and macroscopic buckling is not considered at this time" ([Wallin & Tortorelli 2020, p. 13](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.mechmat.2020.103324); verified). The stability checks therefore extend both sources and deviate from neither.

### The constituent never loses ellipticity in compression, so every instability is structural

The right macroscopic operator is the full, consistent 4×4 nominal tangent 𝔸_M = ∂P_M/∂F_M, including its initial-stress part and with no minor-symmetry reduction, and the right test is **rank-one convexity, not positive definiteness**. Objectivity explains why. Differentiating W(QF) = W(F) twice along a rotation exp(sΩ) gives (ΩF):𝔸:(ΩF) = θ²·J·tr σ in 2D, so 𝔸, and the equally objective 𝔸_M, are indefinite whenever the mean Cauchy stress is compressive, for any material. Because ΩF has rank two, that indefiniteness never reaches the rank-one test (inference, derived; [Zhang et al. 2021, Eqs. 44 and 54](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.02371)).

For the FEAD2026 energy the acoustic tensor has a closed form. With the code's tangent ([NEMOpt.jl L281–299](../Full_Domain/NEMOpt.jl#L281)), Q(n) = μI + c·m⊗m with m = F⁻ᵀn and c = λ + μ − λ ln J, so that **min over n of λ_min(Q) = min(μ, μ + c/σ_min²)**, where σ_min is the smallest singular value of F. The derivation is an inference, confirmed numerically: it agreed with a 0.125° angle scan to 1.7e−13 relative over 200 random deformation gradients (re-computed), and with a 3,600-angle scan on six states in the original Julia experiment ([exp2.jl](../research_notes/NEMOpt%20buckling%20and%20stability%20addendum/evidence/stability_experiments/exp2.jl)). Under compression J < 1, so c > 0 and Q ≥ μ = 38.46 for E = 100 and ν = 0.3; ellipticity is first lost in uniaxial tension at stretch exp(1 + 2μ/λ) = 10.31. The blended low-density law g·(H_Steep·𝔸 + (1 − H_Steep)·ℂ) is a positive combination of strongly elliptic tensors and stays elliptic too; over 200 random blends, min Q/g equaled μ (re-computed). **Any loss of ellipticity of 𝔸_M at the published strains, at most 7.5%, is therefore purely structural,** and the closed form doubles as an exact unit test of the ellipticity module (K-S1 and K-S2 below).

The checks must run along the ramp, not only at F_M, because an instability can occur at t_c < 1 even when the state at t = 1 looks benign. The legacy code converges at ten load steps, t = 0.1 to 1.0 ([NEMOpt.jl L377–396](../Full_Domain/NEMOpt.jl#L377)), which fixes the in-loop resolution; audits refine t_c by bisection.

### The half cell sees only half of the k = 0 spectrum

The half-domain code imposes point-symmetric pairs on its cut edges (D1 entries of −1) and periodic pairs on its side edges (+1), and it prescribes six nodes, which are the four distinct C2 centers on its boundary counted with their periodic images ([NEMOpt-DR.jl L242–299](../Domain_Reduction/NEMOpt-DR.jl#L242); verified). A 180° rotation acts on fluctuations as (g·w)(y) = −w(−y), and it leaves invariant the cell with its even, quadrant-mirrored density, the isotropic constituent and every F_M. K_red therefore commutes with g and splits exactly into an **odd block** of fields with w(−y) = −w(y), which is the half-cell space, and an **even block** of fields with w(−y) = w(y), which contains the translations. Sylvester's law then gives n_neg(K_red) = n_neg(K_odd) + n_neg(K_even) (inference, standard representation theory; [Healey 1988](https://doi.org/10.1016/0045-7825(88)90049-7)). A re-computation on a heterogeneous C2-symmetric 10×10 periodic cell, a linear-elastic surrogate shifted to be indefinite, confirmed the identity at all six shifts tested; at the lowest shift **two of the three negative modes lay in the even sector**, which no half-cell Cholesky can ever see ([parity_inertia_check.py](../research_notes/NEMOpt%20buckling%20and%20stability%20addendum/evidence/synthesis_checks/parity_inertia_check.py); re-computed).

Two further consequences follow (inference). The χ problems are driven by uniform macroscopic increments, which are C2-invariant, so the χ fields are odd and **𝔸_M depends only on K_odd⁻¹ on both domains**; neither ν nor the ellipticity test can signal a symmetry-breaking one-cell instability, even on the full cell. And at general k, C2 maps k to −k, which time reversal already does, so only the four TRIM points split by parity.

| Model | Equals the full-cell symmetric branch | Physically valid on | What it can certify by itself |
|---|---|---|---|
| Full cell (one periodic cell) | — | [0, t_c), where t_c is the first failure of Tier 0a, 0b or 2 | k = 0 in both sectors (Tier 0a) and k → 0 (Tier 0b); k ≠ 0 needs Tier 2 |
| Half cell (point-symmetric) | At every converged state ([Palais 1979](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01941322)), up to an odd-sector limit point where both diverge | The same [0, t_c), which it cannot locate alone | Only the odd k = 0 sector (Tier 0a) and k → 0 (Tier 0b); Tier 1 needs the reconstructed full cell |
| Either, beyond t_c | Report "principal branch unstable" | — | No homogenized property may be reported; post-bifurcation response needs supercells or branch following, outside first-order one-cell homogenization |

The rule is therefore the same for both domains: one-cell and half-cell homogenized results are physically valid **only on [0, t_c)**. The half cell is not less valid than the full cell; it is less able to tell where t_c lies.

## The published cases carry unequal and untested risk

NEMOpt's auxetic optima sit close to a mechanism. In a 2D isotropic idealization the article's ν = 2𝔸₁₁₂₂/(𝔸₁₁₁₁ + 𝔸₂₂₂₂) reduces to (κ − μ)/(κ + μ), so **ν ≈ −0.9 implies κ/μ ≈ 0.053**, close to the dilatational mechanism of rotating units (inference); the article itself describes "internal flexible 'hinge-like' regions that allow rotation" ([Güven & Özdemir 2026, §5.2](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.finel.2026.104599); verified). This near-mechanism character does not threaten macroscopic ellipticity: in 2D isotropy Q(n) has eigenvalues μ and κ + μ, so κ → 0⁺ leaves the margin near μ. What it approaches is the traction-controlled dilatational boundary κ = 0, which does not govern NEMOpt's strain-controlled cells (inference). The objective therefore drives designs toward exactly the regime where Tier 0b is uninformative and Tiers 1 and 2 matter, and optimized periodic structures are known to develop clustered critical modes there ([Wang & Sigmund 2021](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jmps.2021.104415); verified from preprint). Finite-strain auxetic optima checked afterwards have shown short- and long-wavelength instabilities "leading to a change of periodicity of the microstructure" ([Zhang & Khandelwal 2019](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cma.2019.07.027); verified at abstract level), and mechanism-based metamaterials show stiffness that varies non-monotonically with system size, which questions first-order homogenization itself as κ/μ → 0 ([Coulais et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1038/nphys4269); verified at abstract level). One cheap flag carries over from the earlier work: the tangent ν obeys \|ν\| < 1 only while the normal block of 𝔸_M stays positive definite, so a computed ν ≤ −1 signals a near-instability, not a better auxetic (inference).

If NDR-0003 recovers the original inputs, the audit is re-run on them. A re-analysis of digitized article figures is possible, but colormap, resolution and gray levels make it weak evidence, to be labeled as such (inference). An unstable regenerated optimum would be a scientific finding about the formulation, worth reporting; it would not be a reproduction failure, because links E1–E4 concern sameness, not stability.

## Read-only observers add every check without moving a bit of FEAD2026

### One hook, four triggers and one bitwise gate

Diagnostics are a pure function, `diagnose(state::ConvergedState, cfg::StabilityConfig) -> StabilityReport`. The `ConvergedState` is an immutable view holding the design hash, F(t_j) and load-step index, U at that step, the cached Gauss-point 𝔸 and P (NDR-0017), the factor handle where one exists (NDR-0016, NDR-0022), 𝔸_M and the `CellSymmetry`. The observer is called only after `Objective` and `Objective_Grad` have returned, and **it never calls `Objective`**: in the FEAD2026 profile p/β continuation advances per objective evaluation (FEAD2026 value of NDR-0029) and NonconvexMMA's call order must be preserved (NDR-0026), so a single extra evaluation would shift the schedule and change every later iterate (inference from the verified legacy semantics). It draws random start vectors only from a private `StableRNG`, never calls `BLAS.set_num_threads`, scopes every CHOLMOD parameter change, copies or refactorizes rather than mutating caches or factors, and assembles Bloch operators itself from the cached element tangents, so it shares no mutable state with the main assembler.

The whole feature is accepted by one Regime-B gate, **R-S0: every FEAD2026 golden master and as-shipped trajectory is bitwise identical with all diagnostics enabled and disabled.** Four triggers are configured per profile: each converged load step runs the k = 0 verdict; each evaluation, after the gradient, runs ellipticity and the eigenvalues of 𝔸_M; every N outer iterations and at the end of each continuation stage, the TRIM points and contour run; and final designs, raw and thresholded, receive the grid scan, margins and a t_c bisection. In the FEAD2026 profile all four are opt-in. Tracking ellipticity along the ramp needs 𝔸_M at intermediate steps, which the legacy code never computes; with NDR-0016's reuse of the converged factor it costs four back-substitutions and one homogenization pass per step in the Default profile (inference).

### Inertia gives a three-state verdict, not a boolean

CHOLMOD's documented semantics set the design. Its LDLᵀ factorization handles indefinite matrices only when their "leading minors are well-conditioned (2-by-2 pivoting is not supported)"; for indefinite A, "no guarantee of accuracy is provided, since no numeric pivoting is performed"; and a failed Cholesky sets `CHOLMOD_NOT_POSDEF` ([CHOLMOD User Guide](https://github.com/DrTimothyAldenDavis/SuiteSparse/blob/dev/CHOLMOD/Doc/CHOLMOD_UserGuide.tex); verified). Julia's sparse `ldlt` forces this unpivoted simplicial path ([SparseArrays `cholmod.jl`](https://github.com/JuliaSparse/SparseArrays.jl/blob/main/src/solvers/cholmod.jl); verified), and at NEMOpt's reduced sizes it is also slow.

These timings come from Julia 1.11.7, CHOLMOD 5.2.0 and one BLAS thread in the research container; the coordinator's machine is 1.6–2.4× slower ([exp1.out](../research_notes/NEMOpt%20buckling%20and%20stability%20addendum/evidence/stability_experiments/exp1.out); verified). **LDLᵀ is 10–21× slower than Cholesky, so it must never be the routine solve fallback**, which refines NDR-0022's "logged LDLᵀ/LU fallback". In the Default profile a Cholesky failure at a converged state falls back to UMFPACK LU for the solve, and LDLᵀ runs only as a logged diagnostic that counts negative pivots and reports min\|D\| and max\|D\| as growth indicators. That count matched the exact count at 28 of 28 well-separated shifts on a heterogeneous cell ([exp2.jl](../research_notes/NEMOpt%20buckling%20and%20stability%20addendum/evidence/stability_experiments/exp2.jl); verified). Exact counts are cross-checked by dense Bunch–Kaufman inertia in tests and by MUMPS in an extension, which needs ICNTL(13) = 1 or a single process and symmetric-indefinite mode, because with SYM = 1 "numerical pivoting is switched off" ([MUMPS 5.9.1 guide](https://mumps-solver.org/doc/userguide_5.9.1.pdf); verified). MUMPS reports no inertia for complex matrices, so a complex Bloch matrix H = A + iB uses the real embedding [A −B; B A], whose inertia is twice that of H (inference, standard identity).

**The verdict is three-state.** The reference scale is λ_ref = 4π²μ/N_el², the lowest cell-periodic shear eigenvalue of a uniform solid cell in the identity metric: 0.2373 at 80×80, against a measured 0.2371, with the scaled value converging from 1517.6 toward the continuum 1518.4 ([exp1.out](../research_notes/NEMOpt%20buckling%20and%20stability%20addendum/evidence/stability_experiments/exp1.out); verified). A state is **stable** if Cholesky of K_red − τλ_ref·I succeeds, **unstable** if Cholesky of K_red + τλ_ref·I fails, and **indeterminate** otherwise, at one numeric refactorization (0.02–0.05 s) per attempt. The tolerance τ sits **above round-off**, pre-registered from the measured noise floor as in NDR-0006, and **below ε = 5e−4**, so that positive void-localized soft modes of order ελ_ref never fall into the indeterminate band; a window of 1e−8 to 1e−6 is expected (inference). The degenerate-shift episode in row 2 of the disagreement table is the concrete reason a boolean is unsafe. A Cholesky failure during Newton iterations at a non-equilibrium iterate is not a stability statement; only failures at converged states, and in χ or adjoint solves at the converged U, carry physical meaning, so every log record carries a state tag ([implementation note, Q1](../research_notes/NEMOpt%20buckling%20and%20stability%20addendum/stability_implementation_verification.md); inference).

**Margins come from the deflated, unpinned spectrum.** The pinned tangent has the same inertia as the unpinned one apart from two zeros, so it is exact for the sign, but its spectrum contains two pinned-translation pseudo-modes that sit below the physical ones and drift down under refinement (scaled values ≈ 75.0 → 71.8 between 80×80 and 100×100). On a uniform cell they masquerade as softening; on a heterogeneous cell they understated the true margin ninefold (re-computed). The fix needs no extra factorization: with G the zero-padded inverse of the pinned tangent and P the projector that removes the two translations, **P·G·P equals K⁺ exactly** (1.1e−14 in the check), so shift-invert Lanczos on x ↦ P(G(Px)) returns the true lowest eigenvalues ([deflation_pinv_check.py](../research_notes/NEMOpt%20buckling%20and%20stability%20addendum/evidence/synthesis_checks/deflation_pinv_check.py); re-computed).

The eigensolver choice follows package health. **KrylovKit.jl v0.10.4** (2026-06-23) offers Lanczos for Hermitian maps and `geneigsolve` for definite pencils; **ArnoldiMethod.jl v0.4.0** (2024-02-22) documents a shift-and-invert recipe; **Arpack.jl** last released on GitHub in 2021 and its README points users to the other two ([KrylovKit](https://github.com/Jutho/KrylovKit.jl/releases); [ArnoldiMethod](https://github.com/JuliaLinearAlgebra/ArnoldiMethod.jl/releases); [Arpack.jl](https://github.com/JuliaLinearAlgebra/Arpack.jl); verified). Six eigenpairs took 26–29 solves, about 3–5 factorization-equivalents, with a hand-written Lanczos; library timings remain unmeasured ([exp1.out](../research_notes/NEMOpt%20buckling%20and%20stability%20addendum/evidence/stability_experiments/exp1.out); verified).

### Bloch phases ride on the existing master–slave map

The full-cell D1 maps each slave DOF to its master with a unit entry, and each D2 row records that slave's lattice offset, L1, L2 or L1 + L2 ([NEMOpt.jl L195–203](../Full_Domain/NEMOpt.jl#L195); verified). The Bloch operator is therefore a small change at scatter time: read (n₁, n₂) ∈ {0, 1}² from the D2 row, multiply that slave row of D1 by e^{i(n₁θ₁ + n₂θ₂)} with θ_j = k·L_j, and form K(θ) = T(θ)ᴴ K T(θ) in reduced numbering, dropping the affine D2(F_M − I)L term because perturbations carry no macroscopic jump. This follows the standard finite-element Bloch technique ([Åberg & Gudmundson 1997](https://doi.org/10.1121/1.419652)); its mapping onto NEMOpt's numbering is inference. **The center pin must be dropped for θ ≠ 0**, where Bloch conditions already exclude translations ([Zhang et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.02371); verified). Pinning anyway is a spurious constraint that raises eigenvalues: at θ = (π, 0) the homogeneous cell's spectrum moved from [2.621, 2.621, 9.174] to [2.621, 4.365, 9.174] (verified in exp1 and re-computed). The planned one-triangle assembler (NDR-0021) must therefore keep the center DOFs available and apply the pin as a separate step.

Four standard properties make the module cheap and testable (inference). Time reversal gives K(−θ) = conj K(θ), which halves the zone. At the four TRIM points θ ∈ {0, π}² every phase is ±1, so K(θ) is real, real Cholesky applies, and these points cover the 2×1, 1×2 and 2×2 period-doubling modes. Per-k assembly is a sparse linear combination, K(θ) = Σ over m ∈ {−1, 0, 1}² of e^{im·θ}K_m with the real K_m precomputed once per state, so per-k cost is one complex Cholesky. And the θ = 0 spectrum of a 2×2 supercell must equal the union of the 1×1 spectra at the TRIM points, an exact check of the phase map.

Wave-vector sampling follows the symmetry shared by design and load: the IBZ is "determined by the shared symmetries between the microstructure geometry and the macroscopic stress state" ([Wang & Sigmund 2023](https://doi.org/10.1017/pma.2023.5), preprint [arXiv:2210.00003](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.00003); verified). Both legacy scripts mirror the design about both axes (D2), so under 1.075·I the IBZ is a quarter of the zone, while under the shear-type F_M only C2 survives and the IBZ is a half zone, interior included (inference). Sweeping only the IBZ contour is customary but not guaranteed, because spectral extrema "can occur inside the Brillouin zone" ([Harrison et al. 2007](https://doi.org/10.1088/1751-8113/40/27/011); [Maurin et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijsolstr.2017.11.006), title-level). Sampling therefore runs in three levels: Level 1 visits the TRIM points in real arithmetic; Level 2 adds the contour, labeled "not guaranteed"; and Level 3, for final designs, scans a uniform 16×16 or 32×32 half-zone grid refined near the axes, in the manner of Zhang et al.'s 100×100 grid with three refined strips ([Zhang et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.02371); verified). Every level excludes a small ball around θ = 0 and leaves it to the ellipticity test, because there λ_min(K(θ)) → 0 like \|θ\|² even in stable cells. Its scaled limit must match min eig Q(n) of the computed 𝔸_M, which is the E-S4 consistency test, valid for θ between 1e−3 and 1e−1 (re-computed). Boundary condensation by Haynsworth inertia additivity reproduced the direct inertia at 144 of 144 wave vectors ([exp3.jl](../research_notes/NEMOpt%20buckling%20and%20stability%20addendum/evidence/stability_experiments/exp3.jl); [Haynsworth 1968](https://doi.org/10.1016/0024-3795(68)90050-5); verified), but it pays off only above a few hundred k-points per state, since a direct complex Cholesky already costs 0.05–0.12 s, so it stays optional.

### The ellipticity test is exact and costs microseconds

In 2D, det Q(n) is a homogeneous quartic in n = (cos φ, sin φ), so strong ellipticity holds exactly when Q₁₁(n) > 0 for all n (a 2×2 definiteness check) and det Q(n) > 0 for all n (a₀ > 0, a₄ > 0 and no real root of the quartic in tan φ). The test uses the full 2×2×2×2 𝔸_M, symmetrizes Q only against round-off, and logs the major-symmetry residual of 𝔸_M as a sanity check on the four χ solves (inference). The margin B comes from a 1° scan refined by Brent's method and cross-checked against the root test; the published scan practice is π/720 increments ([Zhang et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.02371); verified), and a naive scan costs 6.8e−4 s per state (verified, exp2). The module also logs the 4×4 Voigt eigenvalues of 𝔸_M, which govern stability under traction control, the dilatational normal-block determinant, which separates an "auxetic near-mechanism" from "macroscopically unstable", and a \|ν\| ≥ 1 flag. NEMOpt stores 𝔸_M in the Voigt order (11, 22, 12, 21) ([NEMOpt.jl L44](../Full_Domain/NEMOpt.jl#L44)), so the index map must be explicit.

### Half-cell results are diagnosed on a reconstructed full cell

The resolved half-cell policy extends the converged half-cell displacement to the full mesh by C2, applying the half-period design offset already verified in the earlier report; asserts that the full-cell residual is at round-off level, which by Palais checks the mapping rather than the physics; assembles the full tangent once; and runs Tiers 0a and 2 there, while Tier 0b needs nothing new because 𝔸_M is unchanged. The even-parity half-cell factorization is implemented first as the metamorphic test E-S5, In(full, k = 0) = In(odd) + In(even). Its construction (flip the −1 C2-pair entries of D1, free the six prescribed nodes, pin one node against translation) must be checked against the half-domain constraint code. The literature offers no shortcut here: point-symmetric homogenization papers report large speedups and say nothing about bifurcation, as with Saavedra Flores & de Souza Neto's "speed-up factors in excess of seven", whose abstract contains no stability statement ([Saavedra Flores & de Souza Neto 2010](https://doi.org/10.1108/02644401011044612); verified at abstract level). The stability limits of the reduction are therefore this addendum's derivation, not a cited result.

### Soft-band and void modes are separated before any verdict

NEMOpt's void branch is the linear law εℂ, which carries no geometric stiffness, so void elements cannot buckle by themselves; this is the same effect that energy-interpolation schemes exploit ([Wang, Lazarov, Sigmund & Jensen 2014](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cma.2014.03.021); [Dalklint et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00158-021-03012-z); verified at abstract level). Two other effects still bias verdicts and margins. In the identity metric, void-localized positive modes of order ελ_ref dominate the low spectrum, leaving the sign intact but making raw margins meaningless, so diagnostics report both the lowest mode overall and the lowest **solid-dominated** mode, defined by its fraction of \|strain energy\| in projected-solid elements against a pre-registered threshold.

The clip and the ρ̃^p blend also jointly create a **soft nonlinear band**. Between the blend threshold ω̂^{1/p} = 0.368 and the clip boundary, an element is fully nonlinear (H_Steep ≥ 0.977 once ρ̃ ≥ 0.39) while its stiffness is pinned at exactly g = ε with g′ = 0. The band first appears at β ≈ 9.5 for p = 3, widens to [0.368, 0.423] at β = 16 and [0.368, 0.462] at β = 32, and always ends at projected density H = 0.0794 ([soft_band_clip_check.py](../research_notes/NEMOpt%20buckling%20and%20stability%20addendum/evidence/synthesis_checks/soft_band_clip_check.py); re-computed, matching the earlier report's 0.368–0.462). Material and geometric stiffness of these elements both scale with ε, so their local buckling strain is independent of g; sandwiched in a compressed interface layer about one filter radius wide, they can host localized modes that neither the clip nor the blend removes (inference; no source tests this exact scheme). Verdicts are therefore reported on both the raw and the thresholded, re-analyzed design, as the earlier report already proposes for property comparisons. Element technology adds a caution: Q4 elements overestimated a column's linear buckling load by about 50% on a coarse mesh ([Ferrari & Sigmund 2019](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00158-019-02253-3); verified from preprint), so verdicts on ligaments only a few elements thick are optimistic, and any published stability claim needs a body-fitted cross-check (inference).

## A constrain-first formulation reuses the Z contraction already in the code

### Constrain now, exploit later

The field has shifted "away from avoiding instability and toward taking advantage thereof" ([Kochmann & Bertoldi 2017](https://doi.org/10.1115/1.4037966); verified), and unit-cell work that exploits snapping and bistability now exists ([Aveline, Santer & Hewson 2026](https://doi.org/10.1002/adem.202503183); verified at abstract level only, method details unverified). Exploitation in a one-cell model, however, needs machinery NEMOpt lacks: path following through limit and bifurcation points, branch switching along the critical mode, supercells or Bloch-aware cells for period-changing modes, and objectives defined on the post-bifurcated branch. Constraining instability reuses NEMOpt's Newton solver, adjoint and Z directly, and it targets a visible gap. The DTU line of buckling-strength microstructure design is linearized, solving [K₀(k) + λK_σ(k)]φ = 0 at a few wave vectors after small-strain homogenization ([Thomsen, Wang & Sigmund 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cma.2018.04.031); [Wang & Sigmund 2021](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jmps.2021.104415); verified), while finite-strain Bloch machinery exists for analysis ([Zhang et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1002/nme.6802)) and for prestrained phononic design ([Dalklint, Wallin, Bertoldi & Tortorelli 2022](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jmps.2022.104849); verified at abstract level). Searches found no density-based topology optimization of finite-strain homogenized cells under Bloch-aware stability constraints, which is an absence of evidence, not proof. **The panel's position is that the next paper constrains instability and that exploitation becomes a later study (NDR-0063).**

### Formulation and sensitivities

The proposed problem keeps the FEAD2026 objectives (ν target, κ, shear) and adds a microscopic stability constraint g_stab = 1 − KS_ρ{λ_i(t_j, θ_l)/λ̄} ≤ 0, aggregated over the lowest modes i, the load increments t_j already computed on the ramp, and the wave vectors θ_l ∈ {Γ, X, Y, M}, with contour points added once the topology settles. It also adds a macroscopic constraint g_E = 1 − KS_φ{B(φ)}/B̄ ≤ 0. In 2D this exact ellipticity term replaces the small-k sample that Wang & Sigmund add "for capturing global shear buckling modes", because the Bloch long-wave limit is the acoustic tensor (inference; [Wang & Sigmund 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.01359)). Aggregating over the whole path, rather than evaluating only at F_M, follows Lindgaard & Dahl's finding that the load level at which a design is evaluated has "a tremendous effect" and that a design whose end load passes a limit point during optimization can end up unstable ([Lindgaard & Dahl 2013](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00158-012-0832-2); verified).

The sensitivities reuse existing code almost verbatim. For a simple eigenpair of K_T(u, ρ̃)φ = λSφ with φᴴSφ = 1, at the equilibrium R(u, ρ̃; F(t_j)) = 0, the design derivative combines the explicit terms φᴴ(∂K_T/∂ρ̃_e)φ − λφᴴ(∂S/∂ρ̃_e)φ with an adjoint term −ψᵀ∂R/∂ρ̃_e, followed by the chain rule through the filter and projection exactly as for 𝔸_M. The adjoint ψ solves K_T⁰ψ = Σ_e ∫ Bᵀ[Z_rho : Re(∇φ̄ ⊗ ∇φ)] dV, where K_T⁰ is the real k = 0 reduced Newton tangent whose factorization already exists. **That contraction is already in the code:** `FindTrialU` builds the 𝔸_M sensitivity from `@tullio Z_Cont[ii, jj] = Z_rho(ii, jj, kk, ll, mm, nn) * ∇χ⁽ⁱʲ⁾[kk, ll] * ∇χ⁽ʳˢ⁾[mm, nn]` ([NEMOpt.jl L542](../Full_Domain/NEMOpt.jl#L542); verified), and replacing ∇χ by the mode gradient ∇φ gives the eigenvalue adjoint. `Z_rho = g·H_Steep·Z` correctly makes the third derivative vanish on the linear branch ([NEMOpt.jl L299](../Full_Domain/NEMOpt.jl#L299); verified). The structure matches Zhang et al.'s Eq. 24 and Appendix A, which give ∂𝔸/∂F for a neo-Hookean model ([Zhang et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.14911); verified from preprint). Each eigenvalue or KS aggregate at each retained load step costs one real back-substitution, for Bloch modes too, because the equilibrium is the real k = 0 state (inference). The ellipticity sensitivity is cheaper still: by the envelope theorem at a unique minimizer, dB/dρ = (m⊗n):∂𝔸_M/∂ρ:(m⊗n), a fixed combination of the 16 components of ∂𝔸_M/∂ρ that NDR-0023's aggregated adjoint delivers in one solve, no more than the ν gradient (inference).

The adjoint term is not optional. Ferrari & Sigmund found that dropping it gives "inconsistent sensitivities", with compliance 0.4%, 1% and 6% worse at P̄c = 0.5, 0.75 and 1.0, breakdown by iteration 150 at P̄c = 2.0, and a dropped term comparable in size to the kept one ([Ferrari & Sigmund 2019](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00158-019-02253-3); verified from preprint). The prerequisite is therefore a verified Z: the earlier report found that the Tensors.jl benchmark never cross-checked Z, so NDR-0020's Z test against automatic differentiation and central differences gates the whole formulation. The stability study also cannot run with the FEAD2026 values of NDR-0027, NDR-0028 and NDR-0029, because a constraint margin cannot be resolved through an objective rounded to 1e−3, a gradient built from a rounded ν, or a p/β change made between value and gradient (inference). Gradients are verified on unrounded functions by Taylor-remainder slopes of 2, which is meaningful only away from eigenvalue crossings.

Repeated and clustered eigenvalues are to be expected. Zhang et al. saw up to 27 eigenvalues within six clusters early in one run, and optimized microstructures show "tightly clustered" critical bands ([Zhang et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.14911); [Wang & Sigmund 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.01359); verified). KS aggregation with ρ > 20, or a p-norm with ρ ≥ 8, rescaled whenever p or β changes, performed as well as individual constraints and makes the aggregate's gradient unique ([Ferrari & Sigmund 2019](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00158-019-02253-3); verified). Symmetric polynomials and Seyranian's directional derivatives are the recorded alternatives ([Dalklint et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jmps.2022.104849); [Seyranian, Lund & Olhoff 1994](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01742705)).

### Artificial modes, the clip and the ρ̃^p blend

The linear-buckling remedy of interpolating the geometric stiffness separately (a floor E₀ in K₀, none in K_σ) does not transfer to NEMOpt, because at finite strain it makes residual and tangent inconsistent and slows or stops Newton convergence ([Lindgaard & Dahl 2013](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00158-012-0832-2); verified). **The consistent remedy is a metric.** Zhang et al.'s diagonal pseudo-mass interpolates from ε̂ + (1 − ε̂)ϖ^{p_s} below a lower cut-off, through a C¹ cubic, to 1 above an upper cut-off, with illustrated values ϖ_l = 0.1 and ϖ_u = 0.2 applied to a p-norm nodal pseudo-density; it removes modes in "void and low-density elements" while leaving physical eigenvalues almost unchanged ([Zhang et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.14911); verified from preprint). Built from projected density, those illustrated cut-offs already cover NEMOpt's soft band, whose projected density never exceeds 0.0794 (re-computed); built from filtered density, they would have to exceed 0.462 (inference). Two further options remain recorded: a higher exponent on the linear branch, p_l > p, which Zhang et al. use "to discourage using low-density values to exploit small deformation kinematics", and their adaptive blend. The ρ̃^p blend itself stays at its NDR-0034 default, because its argument and threshold are Wallin & Tortorelli's Eq. 73 and its soft band is inherited from that scheme.

The clip question is settled by numbers rather than precedent. Every buckling reference read uses a smooth floor ([Ferrari, Sigmund & Guest 2021](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00158-021-02854-x); [Wang & Sigmund 2023](https://doi.org/10.1017/pma.2023.5); verified), and the concern is that the clip's zero gradient prevents a stability constraint from asking for material in void to brace a strut. With projection, however, a smooth floor ε + (1 − ε)H^p gives void sensitivities of only 2.7e−3 at ρ̃ = 0.3 when β = 8 and 4e−15 when β = 32, and the kink the clip introduces is a derivative jump of 0.4–0.7% of the largest g′ at p = 3 (re-computed). Neither effect justifies changing the reference law and confounding the stability constraint's effect with an interpolation change. **The clip therefore stays the default in the stability study and the smooth floor runs as a pre-registered ablation (NDR-0061).** The smooth floor, or an equivalent precaution, is required in only two places: complex-step gradient verification, which fails on the non-analytic `max`, and any Taylor test whose designs straddle the clip boundary.

### Continuation, symmetry and validation

Projection steps change stiffness, eigenvalue ordering and mode shapes discontinuously, so the constraint switches on only after the first β stages, rescales KS and λ̄ at every β change, and refreshes the number of tracked modes periodically, as Wang & Sigmund do every 20 iterations with modes "within 5%" of the critical one; an evaluation on the eroded design is advisable because thin struts are where buckling governs ([Wang & Sigmund 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.01359); verified). The half cell may still solve equilibrium, since the prebifurcation state is symmetric, but eigen-constraints run on the full cell, or on both parity sectors at the TRIM points, and never assume a parity for the modes. Design symmetry (quadrant mirror in FEAD2026; point symmetry or none as options) should be separated from analysis reduction, because imposed geometric symmetry is known to shape failure surfaces ([Thomsen et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cma.2018.04.031); verified at abstract level). Final designs are verified by a post-hoc Tier 0–2 sweep along the full path and by a body-fitted nonlinear re-analysis, the practice of Wang & Sigmund (COMSOL), Dalklint et al. (a conforming mesh) and Ferrari & Sigmund (nonlinear analysis) ([Wang & Sigmund 2023](https://doi.org/10.1017/pma.2023.5); [Ferrari & Sigmund 2023](https://doi.org/10.1002/nme.7309); verified at abstract level). For every design the paper should report the first critical load factor, its wave vector, and its class: cell-periodic, symmetry-breaking, period-multiplying or macroscopic.

## Conclusion

The addendum changes how NEMOpt's published numbers should be read more than how the code runs. The values ν = −0.9008 and −0.9132 are tangent properties of a principal branch that nobody has shown to be stable, and the design objective drives cells toward near-mechanisms, exactly where the cheap macroscopic test is weakest and symmetry-breaking or period-multiplying modes are most likely. Two findings make the problem tractable. The constituent provably cannot lose ellipticity at these strains, so any instability is a property of the geometry, the thing the optimizer controls. And almost every needed piece already exists in the code: the reduced tangent for k = 0, the D1/D2 offsets for Bloch phases, the four χ solves for 𝔸_M and the Z contraction for eigenvalue sensitivities. The stability module is mostly a reinterpretation of quantities NEMOpt already computes, attached where it cannot disturb a single bit of the FEAD2026 profile.

The most informative next experiment is also the cheapest: run Tiers 0 and 1 on regenerated §5.2 case-2 and §5.3 case-2 optima from the bridge release. If they pass with margin, the published formulation gains a stability statement it never had. If they fail, the next paper starts from a documented, reproducible gap, finite-strain Bloch-aware stability constraints for homogenized microstructures, which the literature reviewed here has not yet published. Either way, the half-cell reduction keeps its speed only if its results are re-certified on the full cell, at the cost of one assembly, which is small next to the risk of reporting an optimum that sits on an unstable symmetric branch.
